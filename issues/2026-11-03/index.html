  1. 01

    The setup shapes the score

    A benchmark result is also a measurement of hardware, tools, access, and the task chosen.

    THE TAKEAWAY /

    Fabio Akita's local Qwen test finds that a two-bit build finished his small coding canary as reliably as a larger one while using less memory and time. Claire Vo's nine-task agent comparison finds a work winner, but her agents had different connectors, memories, and operators. Together they make a stronger case for evaluating the complete working setup against a real job than for carrying a single rank into every environment.

    WHY IT MATTERS / Engineering and AI purchasing decisions can go wrong when a model score is treated as portable across machines, tools, and workflows.

    STILL UNCLEAR / Akita's harder canary used three runs per combination on small Python bases; Vo's scoring was subjective and the agent setups were unequal. Neither result establishes a universal ranking.

  2. 02

    The record has a labor cost

    Local accountability reporting depends on persistent access work that a national headline rarely shows.

    THE TAKEAWAY /

    Danya Issawi profiles Iowa reporter Laura Belin, who pursued court action over pandemic-era records and denied press credentials while continuing to cover elections and state government in depth. Reader support now lets her pay herself a salary while keeping her reporting free to read. The profile shows a viable arrangement for one reporter, along with how much legal, reporting, and audience work it asks one person to carry.

    WHY IT MATTERS / Public records and legislative detail become useful to a community only when someone has time and means to obtain, check, and explain them.

    STILL UNCLEAR / This is a Substack-produced profile of a writer on its platform, not an independent study of local-news economics or a model proven to work everywhere.

  3. 03

    Automate the neglected work

    An internal agent setup becomes more useful when it remembers context, tests concrete scenarios, and reserves a clear human review point.

    THE TAKEAWAY /

    In Claire Vo's interview, Figma PM Ezra Mechaber describes a shared repository of 31 skills used to find collaborators, brief work, and test product flows. A browser-based bug bash caught both an extreme input failure and a small grammar defect. The growth experiment loop is still maturing: it has concurrent tests, but the reported big win comes without a published metric, and the intended human gate remains central.

    WHY IT MATTERS / The practical gain is better coverage of routine work that teams often skip, provided results are inspected before they affect users.

    STILL UNCLEAR / The account is a practitioner interview about internal workflows. There is no measured comparison against a default agent, no usage telemetry, and no published lift figure for the claimed experiment win.

O modelo de dois bits venceu no teste que cabia em casa

  • Fabio Akita comparou quatro quantizações do Qwen3.8 Flash-Next numa RTX 5090 e no Strix Halo. No canário difícil de programação, Q2_0 e IQ3_S passaram os 36 testes ocultos nas três rodadas de cada configuração testada; com Strata na 5090, a versão de dois bits terminou em média em 7,2 minutos, contra 12,1 da IQ3_S.
  • Na mesma 5090, o Q2_0 gerou de 223 a 259 tokens por segundo com Strata e de 377 a 400 com Basalt. A comparação troca o conjunto inteiro do motor, inclusive formatos e padrões, portanto não isola uma única otimização. O ganho na tarefa completa foi menor que o salto de velocidade de geração.
  • A versão de quatro bits esgotou memória ou travou antes de completar o teste de qualidade. O estudo mede bases Python pequenas e poucas repetições; não demonstra que dois bits preservem qualidade em projetos maiores ou contextos acima de 100 mil tokens.

WHY IT MATTERS / Para escolher uma IA local, contam a memória disponível, o tempo até o primeiro token e a duração da tarefa inteira. Mais bits ou mais tokens por segundo, sozinhos, não garantem melhor trabalho.

How I AI

Four agents, nine tasks, and a ranking that needs its setup

  • Claire Vo tested four personal agents on nine tasks from Slack replies and inbox triage to coding, video, and shopping. Grok Bot led her overall score, while Muse produced her preferred short clip and family newspaper; Dot and Claude Duet showed stronger coding judgment in her Sentry task.
  • The most consequential failure was a plausible code fix that loosened validation. A fast answer did not satisfy the underlying requirement, and the usable artifact and handoff also mattered in her work tasks.
  • Vo says Muse lacked Slack and GitHub, calendars and memories differed, and two operator agents added another variable. Her percentages summarize a subjective review of her own setups, not a controlled model comparison.

WHY IT MATTERS / A fair trial of an assistant should match its access and tools to the job, then inspect the finished artifact and any code change rather than trusting a leaderboard.

A local reporter keeps pressing for the record

  • Danya Issawi follows Des Moines reporter Laura Belin through election briefings, campaign events, and the Iowa Statehouse. Belin has sued over access to pandemic-era public records and over being denied press credentials, while a campaign has stopped admitting her to events.
  • Belin's reporting concentrates on state and local details others may miss. Her work remains free to read, supported by voluntary subscribers who have enabled her to pay herself a salary; the Iowa Writers Collaborative connects nearly 70 newsletters.
  • The article is published by Substack about a writer on its own platform. It documents her experience and a funding arrangement, but does not establish that reader support can replace local newsrooms broadly.

WHY IT MATTERS / Accountability coverage needs people able to attend meetings, challenge access decisions, and follow a local story long after a national reporter leaves.

How I AI

An internal PM agent earns its place on the forgotten tasks

  • Ezra Mechaber describes Figma's shared PM setup: a repository with 31 skills, organizational context, version checks, and private per-person workspaces. Its recurring jobs include finding reviewers, gathering full Slack-thread context, and preparing artifacts.
  • A browser agent followed a FigJam test plan and found an input failure involving 1,000 emojis and a grammar error dependent on a team name. The agent's findings still need useful reporting and a person to decide what blocks launch.
  • An automated growth loop coordinates data, copy, and engineering agents with a planned review gate. Mechaber reports five concurrent experiments and a promising first result, but gives no lift figure; he also says there is no usage telemetry or successful benchmark against a plain agent.

WHY IT MATTERS / A team can test whether context and explicit scenarios reduce neglected work, while keeping a human decision point before product changes go live.
