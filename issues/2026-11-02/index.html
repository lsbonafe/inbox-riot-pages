THE FRONT LINE

  1. 01

    Recovery is part of security

    Trust can fail faster than teams can replace keys, patch software, or deploy a fix.

    THE TAKEAWAY /

    eMudhra's weak intermediate CA keys made certificate trust a live problem, while critical PHP fixes landed amid observed exploit attempts. Railway's exposure of environment variables forced customers to consider credential rotation while new deployments were paused. The practical test is whether a team can replace its trust roots and secrets, monitor for misuse, and keep shipping a fix during an incident.

    WHY IT MATTERS / Finding a breach quickly is only half a response if the deployment or rotation path is unavailable when it is needed.

    STILL UNCLEAR / The incidents differ in cause and scope. The newsletter's wider claim that AI has shifted the advantage toward attackers is an argument, not a result established by these cases.

The Pragmatic Engineer

Three failures, one awkward recovery question

  • eMudhra issued intermediate CA private keys that turned out to be tiny, guessable numbers. Its preliminary explanation points to a flawed key-generation procedure and a mistaken selection of weaker keys.
  • PHP's critical fixes arrived after exploit attempts had been observed. Railway said some environment variables may have been decrypted in at most 1.85% of workspaces, then paused deployments while existing applications kept running.
  • The Pragmatic Engineer argues that AI may speed up attackers. These incidents do not measure that claim; they do show why patching, credential rotation, and deployment recovery must work under pressure.

WHY IT MATTERS / A team may know which credentials to revoke and still be unable to roll out their replacements.

Craft Better Software

An agent skill is a folder, not a giant prompt

  • Daniel Moka's seven tips begin with progressive disclosure: keep SKILL.md short and link to scripts, reference files, and examples that load only when needed.
  • Put repeatable mechanics in scripts and judgment in instructions. Describe a skill in the words a user would use to request it, record real failures in GOTCHAS.md, and show one finished output instead of piling on generic rules.
  • He also recommends deleting instructions that change nothing and keeping shared skills in one place. This is practitioner guidance with no comparative test of the tips, packaged with a course promotion.

WHY IT MATTERS / A team can try the structure on a real task and track whether the same failures recur; the number of files is not the point.

READS
School of the Unconformed

The walking essay's challenge is twenty minutes a day

  • Ruth Gaskovski and Peco describe roughly 20,000 kilometres of walking over two decades, most of it on ordinary neighbourhood routes. Their point is the room a slower pace makes for thought, conversation, and noticing a place.
  • They gather research on health and cognition without insisting on a scenic trail: city and suburban walks count. Their practical invitation is at least twenty minutes a day in November, with less digital distraction.
  • Some of the cited studies show associations rather than causes, and walking as resistance to digital capture is the authors' interpretation. They explicitly reject the idea of a cure-all.

WHY IT MATTERS / The experiment needs a route and a little time, rather than another app or a trip to the mountains.
