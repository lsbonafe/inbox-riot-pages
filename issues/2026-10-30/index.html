THE FRONT LINE

  1. 01

    Who checks the agents?

    As agents touch code, accounts and infrastructure, trustworthy output needs observable actions, constrained access, tests and a person who can stop release.

    WHY IT MATTERS / A fast demo is cheap; the recovery path after a mistake decides whether a team can put an agent to work.

    STILL UNCLEAR / The case studies are specific incidents and practitioner reports, not an industry-wide failure-rate estimate.

    • A research agent found the DNS gap
    • Give agents a way to check their work
    • AI on call is mostly an investigator so far
    • Warp’s factory runs from request to proof
  2. 02

    What does the AI boom cost?

    AI coding adoption and infrastructure spending are visible, while net returns depend on quality, financing and the work a tool actually saves.

    WHY IT MATTERS / Budgets have to survive both a model invoice and a capital-intensive data-center cycle.

    STILL UNCLEAR / Company case studies and market forecasts do not prove a general productivity gain or predict a financial correction.

    • Measure AI coding against quality, too
    • The AI coding bill is easier to count than the return
    • The data-center bet has a break-even question
    • The AI buildout has a geopolitical funding risk
  3. 03

    Safety is already ordinary

    The effects of AI systems appear in surveillance, companion products, local bias and security incidents, far outside the doomsday frame.

    WHY IT MATTERS / People need accountability for the actions and omissions of systems operating now.

    STILL UNCLEAR / Several reports were accessible only as newsletter summaries; the zine attributes their claims and avoids unsupported rates or legal outcomes.

    • A virtual border wall was still a physical safety failure
    • Safety includes ordinary harm
    • Caste bias is a test of local model safety
    • A model gateway release carried a credential stealer
  4. 04

    Keep room for human attention

    Faster creation changes the value of judgment, listening, reading and the relationships that make work and culture matter.

    WHY IT MATTERS / Productivity claims have less meaning if they erase the time to decide what is worth making or reading.

    STILL UNCLEAR / These are essays and interviews, so their insights are prompts for reflection rather than measured universal effects.

    • George Saunders wants the messy first draft
    • Poetry asks for a different kind of attention
    • The scarce input may be taste
    • A comic essay calls friendship necessary and awkward
