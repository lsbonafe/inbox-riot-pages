# Issue format

Each published issue is a JSON file in `src/data/issues/` named `YYYY-MM-DD.json`. Store only edited public content.

Required fields: `title`, `date`, `edition`, `dek`, `editorNote`, `leadTopics`, and `stories`. Each lead topic is a ranked theme with a stable `id`, `title`, central `claim`, substantial `takeaway`, `whyItMatters`, `uncertainty`, and one or more supporting `storyIds`. The takeaway synthesizes the supporting work in two to four sentences rather than repeating the claim. Use 3–5 leads for a large issue; a smaller weekday edition may use fewer.

Each story needs a stable public `id`, `section`, `headline`, `sourceName`, `takeaways` (at least one specific finding), and `whyItMatters`. Set `treatment` to `feature` for deeper coverage or `brief` for a compact, sourced takeaway; omitted treatment defaults to `feature`. Every Read also needs `sourceLanguage` as a BCP 47 tag, such as `en` or `pt-BR`. Write its editorial headline, takeaways, relevance, and any excerpt in the original article's language; for an email-only full work, use the work's language. A newsletter wrapper in a different language does not change the article's language. Both feature and brief Reads follow this rule. The page shows a short `ORIGINAL / PT-BR` style badge only for non-English Reads. Mixed-language lead topics and shared section labels may remain in the issue's editorial language. Reads may use `beat` to group a large issue: `engineering`, `economics`, `accountability`, `frontier`, or `culture`. Use `sourceUrl` for a canonical public article or `emailReference` for an email-only publication; never put a mailbox message ID or private mailbox URL in either. Optional `relatedLinks` holds labeled canonical public URLs for underlying studies or other material needed to assess the takeaway. Keep the original article as `sourceUrl` whenever it exists. Optional `excerpt` must be short and attributed. An `image` needs `kind`, `src`, `alt`, `credit`, and `rights`. `kind: original` uses a local `assets/` path; `kind: source` uses a direct public HTTPS image URL. For SVGs generated by `npm run art:render`, add a supported `motif`, short `label`, and, from Issue 2 onward, `artStyle` with a `template` and `palette` named in `DESIGN_SYSTEM.json`. For example: `"artStyle": { "template": "split-paper", "palette": "copper" }`. Three or more generated images in an issue must use at least two templates and two palettes.

Sections: `reads`, `signals`, `releases`, `offers`, `noise`. Lead topics reference supporting stories without repeating their full text.
Use `reads` for articles and essays where the point is what the writer found or argued, even when the takeaway is short. Use `signals` for specific changes or announcements where the point is what happened and why to pay attention. A short article summary still belongs in `reads`.

Keep the inaugural issue comprehensive. Its opening should still let a reader understand the major topics within a minute. Use an index and clear section headings, then give the strongest stories full cards and the remaining substantive Reads compact briefs so the backlog is navigable.

The validator requires original art for at least three quarters of lead topics, one Read per 20 up to eight, and at least half of Signal Desk items. Every New Releases story needs an image. These are minimums; choose visual subjects for relevance, not to fill a quota.
