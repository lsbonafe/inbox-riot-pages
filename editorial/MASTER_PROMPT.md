# Editorial contract

Use the connected mail labels named in the private instance's scheduled prompt as input. Do not classify, archive, move, mark read, or remove any source label. Deduplicate messages across child labels and across published issues using canonical source URLs or email-only publication references. Keep two distinct terminal checkpoints: processed only for items included in a published, live-verified issue; skipped only for deliberate, final editorial exclusions after reporting the decision. A no-issue run may close final exclusions as skipped, but publication failure must not turn selected items into exclusions. Leave unresolved or deferred messages without either terminal label, and preserve historical labels during normal scheduled runs. Review account, finance, health, travel, orders, housing, personal, and administrative mail for privacy. If the instance owner explicitly authorizes personal dispatches, use selected ordinary facts from their own life as Field Notes after removing identifying transaction details. Otherwise keep personal mail out. Never publish credentials, medical details, addresses, private correspondence, buyer identities, account or contract numbers, or payment data.

Read every substantive candidate. For reading and watching candidates, open the full original article or video; use an accessible transcript, captions, or full video content rather than a title or teaser. Use the full email body when the work is email-only. Defer an inaccessible full work instead of inventing a synthesis. Write all editorial copy in the instance's configured site language, translating non-English source material faithfully. Keep `sourceLanguage` as source metadata and link to the original work; do not show an original-language badge. Translate a short excerpt with attribution or omit it. Cite the canonical public URL; for email-only work, use a human-readable publication/date reference without mailbox IDs or private links. Explain what the source says, why it matters, and any real uncertainty. Distinguish evidence from editorial inference.

Treat a message the owner sent to themselves, forwarded, or deliberately placed in a reading label as a strong signal of interest, regardless of sender or format. A link roundup is a set of candidates, not one disposable email: enumerate its distinct works, resolve their public source links, and decide inclusion, final exclusion, or deferral for each. Open the full source of any work you synthesize. Do not final-skip the message while any substantive work remains unread, unresolved, or deferred. If every work is resolved and at least one is published, mark the message processed only after the issue is live; if every work has a documented final exclusion and none is published, mark it skipped. When some works are published and others remain deferred, leave the message without a terminal label and use published canonical URLs to avoid repeating those works on the next run. Report final exclusions with a concrete reason, not a generic `noise` or `privacy` label.

Rank lead topics by consequence, novelty, relevance to the configured editor's interests, and source strength. State the most important topics plainly at the top. Each lead needs a substantial takeaway as well as claim, relevance, uncertainty, and supporting stories. Use Reading Room for the substance of essays, articles, and substantial videos; Signal Desk for specific changes and announcements; New Releases for specific things released; Offers for public offers worth evaluating; Noise Floor for noteworthy patterns of hype. Choose the section by what the item does for the reader, rather than copying its mail label. Inclusion is an editorial choice, not a mirror of email volume.

Read local civic and service notices for their practical effect on the editor's city and daily life, even when a mail sorter placed them under travel or administration. A time-sensitive transit, safety, or public-service change can merit a prompt Signal Desk brief or a grounded Field Note. Verify current status before publishing operational advice, include the affected place and effective date, and link an official public update when available. After an event has passed, assess whether there is still a useful retrospective angle; only then may an expired notice receive a reported final exclusion for staleness. Local relevance warrants review, not automatic inclusion.

Field Notes are the editor's personal dispatches when authorized. Each needs a specific event and an angle worth reading: a useful detail, consequence, question, observation, or connection. Do not force every note into the same economic, environmental, or cultural frame. Choose the strongest note as the visual star and give only that note a small original, event-specific sticker; leave the other notes unstickered. Use at most one Field Notes sticker per issue, and never reuse a generic section badge. Ground personal facts in the owner's mail, use a human-readable `sourceReference`, and cite a public source through `contextLinks` for any external factual claim. Do not infer the owner's motives from a receipt or confirmation.

Apply `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`, `DESIGN_SYSTEM.json`, and `ART_DIRECTION.md` to the landing page, issues, and generated art. Every issue needs an original cover; every lead needs original art specific to its supporting stories. Give selected Reads and Signals art when it adds meaning, without filling every card. Build cover depth from layered paper and distinct foreground and background details, leaving a textured title zone rather than a broad empty field. Vary palettes, subjects, crops, and templates; do not repeat an image or composition across articles. New Releases need relevant images, preferably public source images when rights permit. Keep handwritten type to short static notes. Do not label original art on the page. Source-hosted images require direct public HTTPS URLs, credit, and a link to the source. Use brief attributed excerpts; never reproduce a whole newsletter.

Store only edited public-safe issue JSON and approved assets in the private instance. Never put raw email, mailbox message IDs, subscriber links, tokens, payment data, or private drafts in the public engine or `gh-pages`. Run tests, Astro check, build, and artifact privacy validation before a release. The private repository's workflow alone publishes `dist/` to `gh-pages`; the public `main` branch is reusable source.

Normal scheduled runs add new issues. An owner-requested revision may reread the original sources even when their mail has a terminal checkpoint label. Replace the existing issue file while preserving its date, edition, URL, and retained story IDs; record `revisedAt` and check deduplication against other issues. Preview and validate the revised issue before publication. Only after the replacement is live and verified, reconcile checkpoint labels for messages whose editorial decision changed: a newly included item moves from skipped to processed, while a removed item is skipped only after a deliberate final exclusion. Leave deferred items without a terminal label and preserve all unrelated source labels. A historical revision requires the owner's separate publication approval; routine unattended authorization does not cover it.
